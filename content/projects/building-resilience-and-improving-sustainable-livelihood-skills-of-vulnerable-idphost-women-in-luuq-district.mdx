# Building Resilience and Sustainable Livelihoods for Vulnerable Women in Luuq

## Hero Summary

CeRID is implementing a five-year resilience and sustainable livelihoods project in Luuq District, supporting vulnerable internally displaced and host-community women to improve food security, strengthen household income, adapt to climate shocks, and participate more actively in decisions that affect their families and communities.

Through climate-smart agriculture, access to productive land and irrigation, livelihood diversification, savings groups, environmental action, and gender-transformative learning, the project helps women build practical pathways toward greater self-reliance and long-term resilience.

**Project status:** Ongoing
**Implementation period:** January 2023 – December 2027
**Location:** Luuq District, Gedo Region, Somalia
**Implementing organization:** CeRID
**Partner:** Trócaire
**Project code:** SOM118

## Project Overview

Communities in Luuq continue to face overlapping pressures from drought, flooding, displacement, insecurity, crop pests, limited livelihood opportunities, and environmental degradation. Women in internally displaced and host communities are particularly affected because many carry responsibility for household food, care, and income while having limited access to land, farming inputs, financial services, and decision-making opportunities.

This project responds through an integrated approach that combines sustainable agriculture, renewable-energy irrigation, livelihood support, community savings, household dialogue, environmental conservation, and institutional capacity strengthening.

The project works with the same core beneficiary groups over several years, allowing CeRID to provide continuous support, reinforce learning, and build deeper and more sustainable results.

## Overall Goal

To strengthen the resilience, food security, economic independence, and adaptive capacity of vulnerable internally displaced and host-community women and their households in Luuq District.

## Key Objectives

1. Improve women farmers’ knowledge and use of agroecological and climate-resilient farming practices.
2. Increase access to agricultural land, certified seeds, farming equipment, irrigation, and renewable energy.
3. Diversify household income through livestock, small businesses, savings groups, and cash-for-work opportunities.
4. Strengthen women’s participation in household financial planning, shared decision-making, and community leadership.
5. Improve community-led natural resource management and environmental protection.
6. Strengthen CeRID and local stakeholders to deliver accountable, inclusive, and sustainable programming.

## Who the Project Supports

The project prioritizes vulnerable households in internally displaced persons camps and host communities, including women-headed households, families affected by drought, flooding, conflict, or displacement, households with malnourished children, socially excluded community members, and women with limited access to farmland, productive assets, or sustainable income.

Beneficiaries, community leaders, women’s groups, youth, elders, government representatives, and relevant ministries are involved in project planning, implementation, and feedback.

# What the Project Does

## 1. Climate-Smart and Agroecological Farming

Women farmers receive practical training and continuous field coaching through a Farmer Field School approach. Training covers soil and water conservation, soil bund construction, mulching, crop rotation, intercropping, organic soil improvement, efficient irrigation, ecological pest management, biopesticide preparation, crop maturity, post-harvest handling, and kitchen gardening.

A demonstration plot in Zone Zero provides a hands-on learning space where women practise vegetable production and learn how to establish household kitchen gardens.

## 2. Agricultural Inputs and Productive Assets

The project provides certified and treated seeds selected in consultation with women farmers. Supported crops include maize, sorghum, cowpeas, Sudan grass, sunflower, spinach, tomatoes, hot chilli, green pepper, coriander, lettuce, banana, and pawpaw.

The project also supports irrigation pipelines, water-distribution systems, nursery and training structures, seed boxes, protected nursery sites, farm tools, shared equipment, backup pumps, and technical field coaching.

When extreme heat reduced germination, the project introduced seed boxes and compost-based nursery sites, improving seedling growth and crop establishment.

## 3. Access to Agricultural Land

The project leases six hectares of farmland for internally displaced and host-community women. The farm is close to the targeted communities and near Luuq’s main market, making it easier for farmers to reach their plots, transport produce, and connect with buyers.

The farm is organised into specialised production zones:

- **Zone 1:** Vegetable production
- **Zone 2:** Sorghum, cowpea, and maize
- **Zone 3:** Banana, pawpaw, and protective cover crops
- **Zone 4:** Mixed food crops, fodder, and soil protection
- **Zone Zero:** Water, nursery, training, sanitation, and demonstration infrastructure

Shade and moringa trees are planted between zones to improve the farm microclimate, protect soil, and reduce erosion.

## 4. Solar-Powered Irrigation and Water Management

CeRID installed a solar-powered irrigation system to provide a cleaner and more reliable water source for the beneficiary farm. The system reduces dependence on fuel-powered pumps, supports cultivation across farm zones, and contributes to climate adaptation.

Water-management activities include canal rehabilitation, construction and reshaping of farm beds, improved water-distribution methods, additional pipelines and hose connections, water-conservation training, flood-protection measures, and maintenance support from selected community workers.

## 5. Cash-for-Work and Farm Rehabilitation

Cash-for-work activities provide short-term income while restoring productive infrastructure. Participants support land clearing, reconstruction of beds and zones, canal rehabilitation, soil levelling, trench and water-channel construction, nursery and training-hall rehabilitation, and protection of farm infrastructure.

These activities helped women restore farmland damaged by El Niño flooding while earning income during periods before crops were ready for harvest.

## 6. Livelihood Diversification

To reduce dependence on a single income source, the project supports women with livelihood options chosen through beneficiary consultation.

- **43 women received livestock**, with each beneficiary receiving four female goats.
- **57 women received small-business start-up kits** to establish or strengthen income-generating activities.

The livestock supports milk production, herd growth, breeding, and future sales. Small-business kits help women diversify household income and strengthen economic resilience alongside farming.

## 7. Village Savings and Loan Associations

Women farmers are supported to form and manage Village Savings and Loan Association groups. Training covers group formation, leadership, elections, member responsibilities, regular saving, internal lending, repayment, meeting procedures, record keeping, and financial planning.

By the end of the 2024 reporting period, the groups had collectively saved **$2,800**, demonstrating growing financial discipline, mutual support, and access to small internal loans.

## 8. Gender Equality and Household Resilience

The project uses Transformative Household Methodology and the Gender Action Learning System to strengthen household cooperation and women’s participation in decision-making.

Sessions address household budgeting, shared planning, gender roles, conflict resolution, joint decision-making, household communication, mobile savings, expense tracking, and community participation.

In 2024, the training reached **200 participants—100 women and 100 men**. Participants reported stronger recognition of women’s roles in farming, business, and household decisions, as well as improved communication between household members.

## 9. Energy-Efficient Cooking

The project distributed **100 energy-efficient Jiko stoves** to women farmers and provided practical guidance on safe and effective use.

The stoves require less firewood, produce less smoke, reduce household fuel costs, lower exposure to harmful fumes, and reduce pressure on trees and natural resources.

## 10. Natural Resource Management and Environmental Action

CeRID supports Natural Resource Management committees and local stakeholders to identify and address deforestation, improper use of farm chemicals, river pollution, closure of watering points, soil erosion, limited market access, and dependence on fuelwood and fossil-fuel irrigation.

Activities include advocacy training, participatory planning, public-awareness sessions, tree distribution, community meetings, cleanup campaigns, and collaboration with local authorities.

A two-day citywide cleanup campaign mobilised **66 community volunteers**, while an environmental conservation meeting brought together **56 participants** from the community, district authorities, and local leadership.

## 11. Environmental Awareness and Climate Action

### Earth Day

Under the theme **“Planet vs. Plastic,”** the project organised a community discussion with 70 participants, environmental awareness sessions in two schools, outreach to **1,956 students**, and a women-led cleanup campaign targeting markets and water points.

### World Environment Day

CeRID convened **104 participants** from communities and local institutions. Each participant received three shade-tree seedlings to encourage household tree planting and climate action.

# Emerging Impact

The project is contributing to visible changes in household and community resilience:

- Women are applying improved farming, irrigation, and ecological pest-management techniques.
- Vegetable and staple crops are supporting household nutrition and market income.
- Women have increased access to land, livestock, business assets, and community savings.
- Renewable-energy irrigation is reducing dependence on fossil fuels.
- Savings groups are expanding access to small loans and collective financial support.
- Men and women are engaging more in shared household budgeting and decision-making.
- Communities are taking greater responsibility for environmental protection.
- Beneficiary feedback is directly influencing irrigation, seed selection, livelihood support, and farm management.
- CeRID’s governance and technical capacity are being strengthened for longer-term programme delivery.

# Community Participation and Accountability

Community participation is central to the project. Women farmers help decide which seeds are purchased, which livelihood options are provided, how savings groups are organised, how irrigation schedules are managed, and what support is needed for pests, water shortages, and farm infrastructure.

Feedback is gathered through field meetings, phone calls, community consultations, and regular interaction with CeRID staff. During the 2024 reporting period, the project received 48 feedback and complaint cases and resolved 47.

# Challenges and Adaptive Responses

## El Niño Flooding

Flooding delayed cultivation, damaged farm zones and irrigation canals, compacted soil, and reduced soil fertility.

**Response:** Farm zones and canals were reconstructed, soil-restoration support was provided, and cash-for-work activities enabled women to earn income while restoring the farm.

## Drought and Water Scarcity

Low rainfall and pressure on water resources threaten crops and livestock.

**Response:** Solar irrigation, water-conservation practices, canal rehabilitation, improved distribution, and dedicated fodder-production areas are strengthening drought preparedness.

## Conflict, Displacement, and Farm Security

Clan conflict disrupted movement and farming, while displacement increased pressure on agricultural land and contributed to theft.

**Response:** Community workers were assigned to support farm security, local security actors increased patrols, and fencing and access arrangements were strengthened.

## Crop Pests and Extreme Heat

Papaya leaf mealybugs, fall armyworms, and heat-related germination failure affected production.

**Response:** Farmers were trained to produce ecological biopesticides using locally available materials, while seed boxes and improved nursery techniques increased germination.

## Limited Tools and Infrastructure

Flooding damaged tools and farm infrastructure.

**Response:** Existing tools were shared, work periods were adjusted, irrigation materials were added, and damaged nursery, training, and canal infrastructure was rehabilitated.

# Sustainability Approach

The project promotes long-term sustainability through:

- Multi-year accompaniment of the same beneficiary communities
- Farmer Field School learning and daily field coaching
- Demonstration plots and household kitchen gardens
- Renewable-energy irrigation
- Ecological pest-control practices
- Crop diversification and fodder production
- Livestock reproduction and small-enterprise development
- Community-managed savings and lending groups
- Natural Resource Management committees
- Collaboration with local government and the Ministry of Agriculture
- Beneficiary-led planning and feedback
- Strengthening CeRID’s policies, strategy, staff, and programme systems

# Coordination and Partnerships

CeRID works closely with Trócaire, internally displaced community leaders, host-community representatives, Luuq District Administration, Jubbaland Ministry of Agriculture, Natural Resource Management committees, women’s groups, SHRA, Food Security and Livelihood Cluster partners, schools, religious leaders, elders, and youth representatives.

# Project Status and Next Direction

The project remains **ongoing through December 2027**. CeRID will continue to accompany women farmers and their households, strengthen climate-resilient food production, improve water efficiency, support savings and diversified livelihoods, reinforce environmental action, and adapt activities in response to beneficiary feedback and changing local conditions.

Priority areas for continued implementation include improving water conservation and drought preparedness, maintaining solar irrigation, expanding agroecology and pest-management coaching, supporting livestock and women-led businesses, strengthening savings groups, improving farm security and infrastructure, and deepening community-led environmental advocacy.
