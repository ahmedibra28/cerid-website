# Cross-Border Community Resilience Project

## Hero Summary

CeRID implemented the Cross-Border Community Resilience Project to strengthen livelihoods, social cohesion, peacebuilding, and the equitable management of shared natural resources among vulnerable communities in Beled Hawa, Dollow, and the wider Mandera Triangle.

The project supported youth, women, farmers, traditional leaders, civil society actors, and local authorities through vocational education, entrepreneurship support, climate-sensitive agriculture, keyhole gardening, leadership development, conflict mediation, advocacy, and cross-border dialogue.

**Project status:** Completed
**Implementation period:** March 2023 – May 2024
**Primary locations:** Beled Hawa and Dollow, Somalia, with cross-border participation from Kenya and Ethiopia
**Implementing organisation:** CeRID
**Partner programme:** Cross-Border Community Resilience
**Funding partner:** Chemonics International / USAID
**Grant agreement:** CBCR-025-FAA-CeRID-23

---

## Project Overview

Communities in the Gedo and Mandera Triangle region face interconnected challenges, including youth unemployment, limited access to vocational skills, displacement, climate shocks, pressure on shared resources, and recurring tensions among communities that depend on water, grazing areas, forests, and cross-border trade.

Women and young people experience particularly high barriers to employment, business ownership, leadership, and civic participation. Farmers also face flooding, unpredictable rainfall, weak irrigation systems, limited access to markets, and reduced agricultural productivity.

The project responded through an integrated model that combined economic opportunity, agricultural support, social cohesion, leadership development, peacebuilding, and cooperative natural resource management.

All activities and milestones planned under the grant were successfully completed.

---

## Overall Goal

To strengthen the economic, social, and environmental resilience of vulnerable cross-border communities through inclusive livelihoods, peacebuilding, leadership development, and equitable management of shared natural resources.

---

## Project Objectives

1. Expand conflict-sensitive and inclusive livelihood and employment opportunities for youth and women.
2. Strengthen farmers’ production, business, and climate-adaptation capacities.
3. Build social cohesion through peacebuilding, mediation, advocacy, and community leadership.
4. Increase women’s and youth participation in local decision-making.
5. Improve conflict-sensitive and equitable sharing of natural resources across border communities.
6. Strengthen collaboration among communities, traditional leaders, civil society, and local authorities.

---

The project directly supported and engaged:

- Vulnerable young women and men
- Unemployed and under-employed youth
- Women seeking income-generating opportunities
- Farmers in Beled Hawa and Dollow
- Women in Qansahley IDP camp

- Women and youth leaders

# Project Components

## 1. Vocational and Technical Skills Training

CeRID provided market-relevant vocational training to 30 vulnerable youth and women.

Training areas included:

- Tailoring
- Electrical installation and maintenance
- Plumbing and drainage
- Beauty and salon services
- Massage and therapy services

The training was delivered through Sayid Mohamed Technical Education College in Beled Hawa.

All 30 participants successfully completed the programme and began applying their skills in employment or self-employment. Graduates reported average daily earnings of approximately USD 15–20.

The benefits extended beyond the individual graduates, improving household income, food security, and access to education and other essential needs.

---

## 2. TVET Start-Up Kits

All 30 vocational graduates received field-specific start-up kits.

The kits included equipment and supplies such as:

- Sewing machines and tailoring materials
- Electrical tools
- Plumbing tools and fittings
- Beauty and salon supplies
- Therapy-related equipment

The kits enabled graduates to transition quickly from training into employment or business.

Some graduates expanded their businesses and created additional employment. One tailoring graduate, for example, hired an assistant after demand for her services increased.

---

## 3. Business, Entrepreneurship, and Civic Skills

Vocational graduates also received training in:

- Business planning
- Entrepreneurship
- Financial management
- Marketing
- Customer service
- Civic engagement
- Business growth
- Managing small enterprises

This component ensured that participants did not only acquire technical skills, but also understood how to operate sustainable businesses and participate constructively in community life.

---

## 4. Farmer Training and Agricultural Support

The project trained 60 farmers in Beled Hawa and Dollow.

Training covered:

- Forage and fodder production
- Post-harvest handling
- Agricultural marketing
- Business management
- Crop diversification
- Climate adaptation
- Irrigation management

Thirty farmers participated in each district.

The project also provided seven irrigation pumps to help farmers manage water access and respond to climate-related shocks.

Before major flooding, supported farmers cultivated approximately 40 hectares and harvested around 3,000 bales of fodder. Farmers later diversified into onions, tomatoes, and other vegetables in response to changing environmental and market conditions.

The agricultural intervention was estimated to benefit approximately 360 people.

---

## 5. Keyhole Gardening for IDP Women

CeRID trained and supported 20 women from Qansahley IDP camp in keyhole gardening.

The training included:

- Garden construction
- Site selection
- Soil preparation
- Water-efficient gardening
- Vegetable production
- Garden maintenance
- Household nutrition
- Sale of surplus produce

The women established small gardens in their home compounds and, in some cases, expanded cultivation to nearby farms.

The activity helped improve access to nutritious food and created opportunities for additional household income. It was estimated to benefit approximately 120 people.

---

## 6. Peacebuilding and Conflict Management

CeRID trained 47 peace-oriented traditional elders from Beled Hawa and Dollow.

The training covered:

- Conflict analysis
- Mediation
- Negotiation
- Dialogue facilitation
- Community reconciliation
- Social cohesion
- The role of traditional leadership in conflict prevention

After completing the training, the elders actively supported peacebuilding initiatives and worked with local authorities and community stakeholders.

They successfully mediated and resolved three land-related disputes, contributing to peaceful coexistence and stronger community relationships.

---

## 7. Advocacy and Civic Engagement

The project organised advocacy workshops for 40 participants from civil society, local government, community leadership, women’s groups, and youth networks.

The workshops focused on:

- Stability
- Good governance
- Peaceful neighbourhoods
- Equitable observance of rights
- Community advocacy
- Collaborative problem-solving
- Inclusive decision-making

Participants developed actionable plans and became more actively involved in promoting peaceful, accountable, and inclusive community development.

---

## 8. Women and Youth Leadership

CeRID trained 42 women and youth in leadership and civic participation.

The training covered:

- Leadership
- Decision-making
- Project management
- Conflict resolution
- Community mobilisation
- Political participation
- Public speaking
- Teamwork

Following the training, some youth participants assumed leadership roles in sports and community groups, while others engaged with government institutions and regional development initiatives.

---

## 9. Cross-Border Natural Resource Dialogue

The project brought together 34 participants from:

- Dollow, Somalia
- Dollow, Ethiopia
- Beled Hawa, Somalia
- Mandera, Kenya

Participants included clan elders, women, youth, local authorities, and natural resource stakeholders.

Discussions focused on the shared management of:

- Water
- Grazing land
- Forests
- Agricultural land
- Other cross-border resources

Participants proposed:

- Joint monitoring and enforcement systems
- Cross-border resource-management agreements
- Community-based natural resource management
- Greater participation of women and marginalised groups
- Joint action plans for fair and sustainable resource use

The meetings strengthened dialogue and cooperation among communities that depend on the same natural resources.

## Hawa: From Training to a Growing Tailoring Business

Hawa Issack Musa joined the tailoring programme to build a livelihood and support her family.

After completing the course, she received a start-up kit containing sewing equipment and materials. She began working from home, built a strong reputation, and later secured a dedicated workspace.

As demand grew, Hawa hired an assistant and began planning to expand into ready-to-wear clothing.

Her experience demonstrates how vocational training, entrepreneurship support, and productive assets can help young women establish sustainable businesses.

## Mohamed: Building a Livelihood Through Plumbing

Mohamed Abdi Ibrahim was responsible for supporting his mother and younger siblings after the death of his father.

He completed a plumbing and drainage course and received a start-up toolkit. The training and tools enabled him to secure regular work, earning approximately 700 Kenyan shillings per day during the early period after graduation.

His income helped him provide food and other essential support for his family.

Saadia, a woman living in Qansahley IDP camp, struggled to provide enough food for her family.

After completing keyhole gardening training, she constructed a garden near her home and began growing tomatoes, kale, spinach, and other vegetables.

The garden improved household nutrition and inspired neighbours to seek her advice. Saadia became a local source of knowledge and encouragement for other women interested in household food production.

Idriss Barre Ahmed completed electrical and electronics training through the project.

After graduation, he entered the labour market with practical technical skills and a start-up kit. He later secured employment as an electrician and maintenance focal person with a telecommunications company.

His story illustrates how market-oriented vocational education can connect young people to professional employment and long-term career growth.

# Challenges and Responses

## Limited Land and Water for IDP Women

Women in crowded IDP camps had limited space and water for keyhole gardening.

**Response:** CeRID helped women collaborate with nearby farmers and encouraged host communities to provide small land allocations.

## Extreme Weather and Flooding

Heavy rainfall and flooding disrupted meetings, cultivation, and fodder production.

**Response:** Activities were rescheduled, irrigation support was provided, and farmers were encouraged to diversify production.

## High Demand for Vocational Training

More than 100 people applied for only 30 available places.

**Response:** CeRID used a transparent community nomination and selection process and linked participants with local training institutions for continued skills development.

## Communication and Decision-Making Gaps

Some women and marginalised participants lacked confidence in speaking and negotiation.

**Response:** Training included public speaking, active listening, conflict resolution, negotiation, and consensus-building.

## Cross-Border Logistics

Travel and coordination across border areas were difficult because of limited transport and infrastructure.

**Response:** CeRID used local networks, improved communication channels, and dedicated logistics planning.

## Slow Decision-Making

Government and traditional decision-making processes sometimes delayed activities.

**Response:** CeRID worked closely with authorities, set clearer timelines, and encouraged institutions to appoint representatives.

## Community Gatekeepers

Some influential individuals attempted to control discussions and limit the participation of marginalised groups.

**Response:** CeRID built trust with leaders while promoting inclusive dialogue and strengthening participants’ confidence.

## Misunderstanding of Advocacy

Some participants viewed advocacy as political or confrontational.

**Response:** Advocacy was presented as collaborative, peaceful, and focused on practical community solutions.

# Key Lessons Learned

- Flexibility is essential in contexts affected by climate shocks and insecurity.
- Strong local partnerships improve access, trust, coordination, and sustainability.
- Practical training is more effective when combined with productive assets.
- Women and marginalised groups need both skills and safe opportunities to participate.
- Climate resilience must be built into agriculture and livelihoods programming.
- Transparent participant selection is essential when demand exceeds available resources.
- Inclusive dialogue can overcome gatekeeping and power imbalances.
- Cross-border resource challenges require cooperation between communities and authorities.
